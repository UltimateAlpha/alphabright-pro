01 — Peniremit

Making a basket of tokens spend like one number

Lead Product Designer · SMC / Penilabs · 2026 · 0→1 · launched April 2026

Peniremit lets anyone pay bills — airtime, data, cable TV, electricity — and send to bank accounts directly with crypto. The hard part was never the blockchain. It was making a volatile, multi-token balance feel as simple and predictable as spending cash.

9,000+
users in first 10 weeks
$400K+
processed in that window
300+
tester community
4 rails
bills, airtime, data, bank

The problem

A user's balance isn't one currency — it's USDT, USDC, Stable Naira and other tokens, each a different size, some volatile. To pay a ₦2,000,000 school fee, a normal person would have to work out which coins to combine, in what order, and hope the rate held. That math is exactly what kills trust in a crypto payment app.

The call I made

Default everything to Smart Spend: the app automatically selects the fewest tokens needed to complete a transaction and denominates spendable value in stablecoins, so a user sees one predictable number. But I refused to make it a black box — the selection is shown as a progress ring (Selected vs Completed against the ₦2M target) and is fully overridable: "We selected the fewest tokens required. You can update your selection anytime."

Predictability for the 90%, control for the 10% — in the same screen.

The tradeoff

Auto-selection risks feeling like the app is deciding for you, and defaulting to stablecoins meant volatile tokens couldn't be the headline balance. I accepted both — and bought the trust back by making every automated choice visible and reversible at the moment of payment, not buried in settings.

The outcome

Peniremit reached 9,000+ users and processed $400K+ in its first 10 weeks, with a 300+ person tester community feeding the loop. The Smart Spend pattern became the spine of the product — it's how a bank transfer, an airtime top-up and a ₦2M bill all feel like the same simple action.

What I designed myself
Hands-on / IC
  • The Smart Spend token-selection flow and its progress-ring logic, end to end
  • Transfer-to-bank and transfer-to-user flows, including the currency toggle
  • Virtual card creation, detail and management screens
  • The home dashboard, bill-pay grid and USD/NGN switching
What I led the team on
Leadership
  • Product vision, roadmap and scope with PM and engineering
  • The "tithing vs spend-and-save" call — reframed a single-faith feature into a flexible set-aside, settled with research across ~30 community and ~20 external users
  • Design-system scoping so the team shipped consistently
  • The 300+ tester-community loop feeding real usage into decisions

02 — Peniwallet

The redesign that cost users money — and what I did about it

Lead Product Designer · SMC / Penilabs · Non-custodial · 5 chains

Peniwallet is a self-custody wallet across BNB Chain, Solana, Ethereum, TON and BASE — send, receive, swap and track assets with the keys in the user's hands. This is the case study I'm most honest about, because the strongest decision I made here started as a mistake.

$1.2B+
cumulative on-chain volume
5 chains
BNB·SOL·ETH·TON·BASE
Non-custodial
user holds the keys
Best of both
the fix, in one phrase

Where it started

Transaction history was originally built per-address — like a chat thread. Every time you sent to someone, you saw your whole history with that address, so you unconsciously recognised "this is my friend's wallet, this is the exchange." It felt non-standard to me, so I scrapped it and replaced it with one clean, consolidated transaction list.

The consequence

After launch, users started losing money. Without the per-address thread, someone would paste a wallet address, drop or mistype a single character, and send crypto to an address that didn't exist. On-chain, that's irreversible. It happened more than once — and it hurt, because I'd treated the change as an obvious cleanup.

A crypto address isn't a phone number. It's 24+ unlabelled characters — nobody memorises it.

What I'd missed

That per-address history wasn't clutter. It was how people verified they were sending to the right place. The "standard" consolidated view was right for the whole world and wrong for our user at the one moment that mattered most. Not everything made for everyone is made for your user type.

The fix

I brought the per-address chat history back — and kept the consolidated ledger too. A unified list for overview; the per-address thread for trust at the moment of sending. I paired it with recently-used and saved addresses so manual entry of 24-character strings became the exception, not the rule.

The decision, side by side

✓ Consolidated ledger — kept

One place to see everything: sent, received and swaps across chains and dates. Great for overview and reconciliation.

✓ Per-address thread — restored

The history with a specific address, chat-style — so before you send, you recognise you've transacted safely with this wallet before.

What I designed myself
Hands-on / IC
  • The transaction-history architecture — both the consolidated ledger and the restored per-address thread
  • The send flow: address entry, recently-used and saved addresses
  • Asset-detail views and the swap entry points
What I led the team on
Leadership
  • Scoping the redesign to meet the competitor bar for self-custody wallets
  • Multi-chain network management across five chains
  • The post-incident process change: mid-workflow design check-ins
  • Backing a direct report's cleaner dashboard when it beat my own — and shipping theirs

03 — Capitalyze

Terminal-grade depth, without the terminal learning curve

Product Designer · 2023 → 2025 · Desktop data platform

Capitalyze is a financial-intelligence terminal for institutional investors — financial statements, custom charts, 13F ownership, insider activity and a 100+ criteria screener in one place. Benchmarked against Koyfin, the brief was blunt: the same depth, but cleaner and faster to read. For an analyst, every extra click is a tax paid hundreds of times a day.

The problem

Institutional research tools win on data and lose on usability. Analysts put up with ugly, dense terminals because nothing cleaner has the depth. Capitalyze's bet was that you could have both — but only if the whole product spoke one visual language, so learning one screen taught you all of them.

The call I made

I built a modular design system as the backbone: one set of primitives — the data table, the metric card, the chart, and a single control bar (period scrubber, unit, decimals, ordering, freeze panes) — reused across Income Statement, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow, Ratios, the Chart Builder and the Screener. Learn the control bar once, and it works everywhere.

Consistency is the feature. In a terminal, a familiar pattern beats a clever one.

Progressive disclosure

The company Overview opens with calm at-a-glance cards — market data, capital structure, profitability, valuation — then lets you drill into full statements, expand disaggregated revenue, or push any metric straight to the Chart Builder. Power lives one click deeper, so the first screen never overwhelms.

Power features, made approachable

The hardest surfaces were the ones built for the most advanced users. I designed each so a first-timer could get a result without a manual — same primitives, same mental model, every time.

04 — Retina-AI

In a diagnostic, the UI's first job is to refuse bad input

Product Designer · RETINA-AI Galaxy™ · Clinical study · not FDA-approved

RETINA-AI Galaxy™ is an AI-assisted retinal screening platform used in clinical settings. A technician captures fundus images; the model grades them for disease. I designed the clinician-facing experience — where the problem isn't delight, it's trust: the system has to catch a bad image, say exactly what's wrong in plain language, and never let an unreliable capture reach a diagnosis.

Patient exams list · triage at a glance by status

The problem

In a diagnostic tool, a bad input produces a dangerous output. Each exam needs four fundus images — optic-disc-centered and macula-centered, for both eyes — often captured by a clinic technician who isn't an imaging specialist. If one is too dark, off-centre or blurred, the model's reading can't be trusted. So the experience's first responsibility is to stop a bad image from ever reaching the model.

The call I made

Quality gating with plain-language reasons. Every capture is checked, and a failure is framed in red with exactly why — "The optic disc location is not centered," "The image is too dark" — never a generic error. The technician knows precisely what to re-shoot. And Send for Diagnosis stays disabled until all four images pass, turning from muted to active only when the set is clean.

The most important thing this UI does is say no — clearly, and at the right moment.

Legibility over density

A 2×2 capture grid mirrors the clinical protocol — each eye against each centring — with thumbnail rails to swap a failed image for a better one, or pull a fresh set from the camera. The exams list gives the clinic triage at a glance: In Progress, Insufficient Quality, Diagnosis Completed, Ungradable — always colour and label, never colour alone.

The outcome

The platform was cleared to run a validation study across 1,100+ patients at roughly 96% accuracy. The quality-gating flow is what makes that number trustworthy at the point of capture — the model only ever sees images good enough to grade.

05 — Chipper Cash

Rebuilding onboarding around trust — and reading the result off the funnel

Lead Product Designer, CX · Payments · US / UK / Africa

Chipper Cash is one of Africa's largest consumer payment apps. I led CX design across onboarding, account statements, customer-journey mapping and regulatory disclosure — work that ran through product, engineering, legal and compliance. The through-line: make a high-trust financial product feel effortless, and measure every change against the funnel.

+4.8%

The redesigned onboarding flow — email gate, consent copy, selfie, BVN/NIN, activation checklist

The problem, in numbers

Full-funnel onboarding completion was just 1% — 438 of 46,000 users reached the end. With no email gate, the top of funnel filled with low-quality and bot signups. 25% dropped before the selfie, because the BVN/NIN screens carried no consent language and bred distrust. 17% abandoned after the liveness check with no progress signposting. Verified users then landed on a bare home with nothing to do — and the USD card never surfaced in-flow, so only 20% of eligible users ever activated one.

The calls I made

An email OTP gate upfront — deliberately trading roughly 4% of raw volume for quality, filtering bots before account creation. Explicit BVN/NIN consent copy placed on the exact screens people were leaving, to rebuild trust mid-funnel. A post-KYC onboarding checklist with a visible progress bar — verify, add money, claim USD card — so no one lands on an empty home. And the USD card surfaced inside the flow, not buried after it.

I didn't guess at the drop-offs. I read them off the funnel, fixed the specific screen, and watched the same funnel move.

The result, measured

Against intentionally lower entry volume, selfie submission rose +4.6%, liveness pass +6.2% and document submission +3.1%. Verified throughput went up, not down — Tier 1–3 KYC verified +3.8% and Tier 2–3 +4.8%. Fewer, higher-intent users — and more of them finishing.

06 — Bitmama

Rebuilding a crypto exchange from the user up

Lead Product Designer, CX · Crypto exchange · mobile + web

Bitmama is a blockchain platform for buying, selling, exchanging, storing and investing in crypto. Users had too much information and too little flexibility. So I started not with screens but with research — why people left, what they valued, and what they actually needed — then rebuilt the experience end to end against those findings.

  • No third-party integrations
  • An inaccurate ETH chart

  • Third-party purchase & fiat exchange

From findings to product

The research pointed straight at the roadmap. I added USDT and multi-currency management, a Bitcoin card, third-party purchase and fiat-exchange flexibility — and built a design system so the product could grow consistently instead of accreting screens. The brief set targets of +35% app usage, +35% transactions and +25% new users.

The redesign didn't just hit the targets — it cleared them several times over.

The outcome

In the two months after launch, app usage rose +45%, the user base grew 400%, and the App Store rating climbed from 2.9 to 3.8. The redesign also contributed to the company's $2M funding round — the clearest signal that the experience, not just the feature set, had changed.

About

A product design leader who never left the craft.

I'm Bright Anyanwu, a Lagos-based product design leader. I currently lead Product and Product Design at SMC / Penilabs, where I own the strategy for Peniwallet and Peniremit and still design the flows myself.

Before this I built across African and global fintech — payments and CX at Chipper Cash, a research-led rebuild at Bitmama, investor tooling at Capitalyze, clinician-facing health-AI at Retina-AI, and operations work at Deel. My throughline is high-trust products: payments, wallets, diagnostics — the places where a design mistake has a real cost, and a good decision compounds.

I also founded Raisen (raisen.org), a solo-built AI career-prep product now past 1,500 users — which is where a lot of my AI-native build practice comes from.

Increasingly I work the way the strongest product-design teams now do — designing and building in the same breath. I prototype and ship in code with Claude Code and Lovable, so an idea reaches something real fast instead of waiting on a handoff. This site is one small example: designed and built by me, end to end.

How I work

Curiosity before the redline

When a designer's work misses the mark, I ask why they made the call before I give feedback — their reasoning is sometimes better than mine. Disagreements get settled with evidence and user research, not hierarchy.

AI-native by default

I design and build with AI in the loop every day — Claude Code for prototyping and real front-ends, Lovable for production builds. It's how I move from idea to clickable fast.

Ship early, loop tight

Distributed team, async by default. I'd rather have the honest conversation on day two than a painful one on day ten — share rough, critique early, iterate. And I own my misses out loud.
